# Gravityborn architecture

The TypeScript simulation runs in the browser and in Capacitor Android/iOS projects. The browser is the primary automated interaction surface. Android has an emulator-verified debug build; physical-device, iOS build and store-release gates remain external. See `native-delivery.md` and the section-by-section `implementation-status.json` ledger.

`GravitySystem` owns all gravitational acceleration. A normalized global vector combines with radius-indexed local fields, then response and safety caps apply. Matter.js's built-in gravity is disabled. Acceleration multiplied by body mass becomes force, so mass changes collisions without changing free-fall acceleration. Definitions specify material, mass, gravity response, faction, tags, health, and breakability.

Entity gravity response combines a base scale with independent named factors. Phasing contributes zero, each parasite owns a separate factor, and theft removes only its own factor on expiry. This prevents out-of-order restoration from erasing another live effect. Recycled projectiles receive a new generation and reset body properties; timed statuses and lock resistance verify generation before changing them.

Gravity Transfer exchanges both momentum and signed gravity response between two movable, responsive bodies for four seconds (subject to derived duration and response caps). A linked body cannot enter another transfer until its link expires. Removing the transfer factor preserves phase, parasite and theft factors applied independently.

`PhysicsWorld` owns the Matter.js engine, body lifecycle, projectile pool, collision facts, and finite-value/velocity limits. It advances at 120 Hz. Incoming velocities are recorded before collision resolution. Physics callbacks record collision facts; gameplay consumes them after the solver finishes. Local fields are indexed by covered spatial cells. Matter.js handles collision broad-phase and rigid body resolution. Both new and sustained contacts produce facts, allowing contact damage to repeat after the player's immunity window.

Balance, entity definitions and derived-stat limits live in `src/data`; enemy and boss behaviors, environment hazards, physical-object fields and run anomalies have separate JSON tuning catalogs. Authored abilities, relics, equipment, classes, rooms and progression content live in `src/content`. Renderers and audio subscribe to typed gameplay events; presentation never determines damage. Seeded runs compose authored chambers, multiwave encounters and branching maps.

Reference APIs: [Matter.Engine](https://brm.io/matter-js/docs/classes/Engine.html), [Matter.Body](https://brm.io/matter-js/docs/classes/Body.html).

## Gameplay and causality

`Game` owns state transitions, AI, ability cooldowns, damage, death, explosions, and run statistics. A gravity change or well creates a chain ID. Impacts propagate that cause to affected bodies. Wells retain their original cause for objects that enter later. Impact damage uses incoming normal speed, interacting mass, and a configurable coefficient; tangential sliding does not count as a high-speed impact. Ordinary unmanipulated settling causes no impact damage, avoiding automatic victories before the player acts. Enemy contact, shots, and explosions remain dangerous.

Explosions are drained from a bounded causal queue after collision processing. Objects are removed before their explosion is processed, so a barrel cannot explode twice. Chain IDs expire, repeated effects are deduplicated, and causal depth is capped. Selecting the already-active gravity direction does not create a new cause. The HUD shows the strongest currently active chain.

The typed event bus also exposes run start/end (including abandonment), power upgrades/evolutions, elite application, boss activation/defeat, resolved collision facts, enemy launches and chain start/extension/end. A launch is recorded once per enemy and causal chain when it exceeds the impact-speed threshold. Chain extensions emit only for distinct accepted effects; expiration and room resets report separate ending reasons. Events persist across world resets without recreating presentation subscriptions.

Player-caused chains of 5, 10 and 20 effects grant a bounded 4–6 second energy-regeneration bonus. Temporary modifiers use simulation-time expiry, survive a same-room build recalculation, and clear on a room reset. Smaller subsequent chains cannot weaken or shorten an active stronger bonus. Environmental/enemy chains do not grant this bonus. The effect strip shows its remaining time; level and XP remain visible in the combat health panel.

The field evaluator supports constant/linear/inverse-square falloff and radial/vortex/directional/zero fields. Powers, bosses, environmental objects and anomalies all use this authority. Field filters match material or entity tags. Burning bodies spread heat through contact; ice quenches them. Broken energy cells and generators discharge through conductive bodies, interrupted by insulating gaps and walls. Material reactions retain causal IDs and have per-frame and depth limits.

`Expedition` owns seeded route transitions, encounters, rewards and run metrics. `RunBuild` owns XP choices, equipped powers, primary-well evolution, relics, equipment, mutations and derived modifiers. `Profile` retains discoveries, research, equipment, classes, challenges and mastery. Breaking a physical rift seal reveals a hidden map route; clearing its optional elite encounter awards rare loot and regional lore. `Tutorial` runs seven manually completed training chambers without altering expedition progression.

`ModifierSet` composes additive, multiplicative and priority override values, tag filters and cooldown-limited event/periodic triggers. Both values and triggers accept conjunctive conditions on health/energy ratios, core speed, stored charge and hostile proximity (240 world units). Missing or nonfinite context fails closed. Unsatisfied conditions do not consume trigger cooldowns. Casts snapshot their conditions before paying energy. Resource ceilings and persistent body properties stay unconditional to avoid recursive thresholds or stale physics properties; the authoring validator enforces this. Conditional relics expose their current conditions in the effect strip alongside shield time and stored charge.

Completed challenges also unlock six optional core shells, eight titles and six short memories. Unlocks derive from validated challenge completion IDs; the profile stores only the selected shell/title, rejecting locked selections on load. Cosmetic data changes outlines, accents and color without affecting simulation, and high contrast overrides its color. The codex names these rewards and records recovered memories.

## Presentation and input

`Renderer`, `Feedback`, and `GameAudio` consume simulation state or typed events. They never determine damage. The Canvas renderer uses distinct silhouettes and symbols, a capped pixel ratio, short trails, and bounded pools. Audio is synthesized after a user gesture and voice-limited. The browser loop uses an accumulator, a fixed physics step, and a maximum catch-up interval.

Impact reactions retain at most 64 short-lived deformation records. Entity outlines squash along the collision normal and stretch with speed without modifying rigid-body geometry. Strong collisions can pause the simulation clock for 32 ms, rate-limited to once per 180 ms; the frame accumulator discards paused time instead of catching it up. Reduced motion disables deformation and hit pause; reduced flashing also suppresses core activation/flip pulses. Debug outlines always retain true physics geometry.

`main.ts` connects semantic DOM controls to the simulation. Keyboard movement uses screen coordinates, which remain stable as gravity changes. Pointer capture supports joystick drags. Gravity buttons handle pointer presses so a second simultaneous touch works; keyboard activation remains available. Blur, visibility loss, and pointer cancellation clear movement. Dialogs pause simulation and remember whether it should resume. Portrait presentation follows the player horizontally; short landscape presentation follows vertically. Neither changes world coordinates. Normal desktop aspect ratios show the centered complete room.

`settings.ts` validates and migrates settings. `SaveStore` uses a versioned checksum envelope and backup recovery, with validated profile and route checkpoint readers. Checkpoints resume from room boundaries, not a serialized rigid-body solver. Browser storage and preloaded Capacitor Preferences share a synchronous cache interface; native writes drain in order. Storage failure is reported without preventing play, and future-version records are not overwritten. The generated service worker caches the production shell atomically for offline browser startup.

Local diagnostics record bounded counts, totals, maxima and the latest 300 gameplay events inside the profile save. Players can disable recording, clear it or export a JSON report. Diagnostic exports omit run seeds and account information; no analytics network service is used. Inspector mutations mark expeditions as assisted.

`RunArchive` stores 20 results and one complete victory ghost in a separate, bounded native/browser record. Quarter-second samples use chamber-relative simulation time and region/depth/node IDs. Ghost playback interpolates positions without creating physics bodies; seed, rules, starting build and replay revision must match. Recording caps at 24,000 samples and 256 chambers. Resumed, truncated and assisted runs save results only. Imports validate size, catalogs, numeric bounds and monotonic samples, receive fresh local identities, are labeled unverified and cannot award progression or local records. Shared JSON includes the final power levels, well level, relics and build bonuses. Historical rotating challenges recover their UTC date from their seed. Bump the replay revision when physics or content changes invalidate recorded routes.

## Content authoring

Gravity Transfer exchanges two eligible bodies' signed gravity response and velocity. Gravity Chain circulates signed response around up to five bodies while launching them. Both own temporary factors with generation-bound expiration; existing transfer-linked, static or zero-response targets are excluded. Flip and rotation attribution compare authoritative samples before/after the direction change, so zero-G pockets cannot falsely redirect projectiles. Training begins with a zero direction and normal strength, making the first directional input physically causal. Replay revision `gravityborn-0.4-r2` includes these changes.

`src/data/regions.json` is the append-only region catalog: each entry supplies its biome palette, enemy pool, guardian, physical props, ambient fields/hazards, moving-wall rule, planet properties, story and secret lore. Existing indexes are persistent save identities, so append new regions rather than reordering entries. Campaign length, endless wrapping, room generation, archive recipes and checkpoint bounds derive from catalog size. A region using registered enemies, bosses and gravity effects needs no engine changes. `validateRegions` checks references, stable IDs, narrative values and environment budgets; `tests/regions.test.ts` exercises a ninth region and expanded class saves. Adding genuinely new behavior still requires a tested gameplay effect, while the gravity/physics authority stays shared.

Add an `AbilityDefinition` to `src/content/abilities.ts` with a stable ID, name, description, rarity, synergy tags, effect, energy/cooldown, radius/strength/duration, target and maximum level. The effect implementation is shared across definitions. Optional parameters configure material/tag filtering, falloff, momentum scaling, planet count, compression, chain length, or field travel speed. Traveling fields update the spatial index and retain one cause across everything they encounter. Optional feedback settings override color, tone frequencies and duration; otherwise tags choose defaults. Field colors inherit the power's feedback color, and directional fields show oriented chevrons. Evolution references point to another authored definition. Mastery objectives, codex entries, inspector selectors and eligible upgrade/shop pools derive from the catalog automatically. Add compatible combinations in the synergy catalog when the mechanic needs one.

Synergies define power-family and/or power/relic-tag prerequisites plus normal modifier and trigger arrays. The matcher follows evolution links with cycle protection, including evolved-only builds. Normal upgrades retain both base and evolved powers. Stable synergy trigger IDs preserve cooldowns through rebuilds. `OnKill` filtering uses killing-effect tags, while victim categories remain available in the typed killed event. Seven combinations include a timed escape shield and bounded impact-kill charge recovery. Active effects are explained in the build panel. Replay revision `gravityborn-0.4-r3` includes these rules.

Use `npm run validate:content` to check unique IDs, numeric limits, evolution/prerequisite cycles, modifiers/triggers, rewards, equipment sets and biome references. A new effect kind needs a focused gameplay test; a new definition using an existing effect is covered by catalog validation and the per-power finite-simulation test. Use the inspector for interactive review, the seeded expedition bot for input-only reachability, and `npm run benchmark` for host simulation budgets. These checks do not establish human enjoyment or mobile-device performance.

## Safety budgets and limits

Endless pressure is authored in `src/data/endless.json`. Chamber count determines the stage; catalog tiers layer environmental fields while the latest directional program replaces earlier rotation/reversal/storm programs. The room generator enables moving geometry at stage 500, and collision pulses keep their existing cooldown and field cap. Depth-based health pressure grows to a finite safety ceiling of 10,000 difficulty units. Active rules appear as status badges. Room rules track entity generations, so pooled projectiles receive mass and restitution changes on every incarnation without compounding. Destroyed planetary sources remove their fields. Replay revision `gravityborn-0.4-r4` includes these changes.

- 120 Hz solver; maximum linear velocity 18 Matter units (1080 px/s), angular velocity 0.3 rad/base-frame.
- Maximum acceleration 0.009 px/ms², maximum impulse delta 16 Matter units.
- 220 live bodies, 50 indexed fields, 36 fired projectiles, 200 particle slots.
- Player wells have their own stricter limit and cooldown.
- Fixed substeps, bounded speeds, and thick arena geometry mitigate tunneling. Matter.js does not provide general continuous collision detection here; future thinner geometry or faster objects require new regression tests or a swept collision solution.
- Physics reproducibility is suitable for local tests, not cross-platform lockstep determinism.

The benchmark isolates simulation cost on the host CPU. It does not establish phone GPU/frame-time performance. See the milestone review for measured results.

Run event definitions may set a persistent run phenomenon or begin an elite/challenge chamber. Combat reuses ordinary room construction and rule configuration while retaining the original event node in the route. Its reward is held only in memory until victory; defeat drops it. Room-boundary recovery returns to the preceding event decision. Successful rewards and the visited node are then checkpointed together. Expedition and build snapshots clone their nested values so later live mutations cannot contaminate an earlier checkpoint. Event authoring validation checks choice identities, bounded numeric costs/rewards, power/mutation/rule references and combat types. Replay revision r6 separates recordings made before these event and core-collision changes.

Gravity lensing belongs exclusively to presentation: up to six nearby/significant radial fields displace the decorative background grid by at most 36 world units. Bodies, walls, aiming, and collision geometry remain authoritative and unwarped. One grid path batches the strokes; up to 64 procedural field motes are shared across active fields. Void-tagged radial powers and strong non-ability singularities get a dark core and an orbital ring. Reduced-motion/low-power modes skip the grid distortion. The benchmark reports lens-coordinate CPU time separately from physics; it does not measure GPU/device rendering.

Compact challenge codes encode a versioned seven-field UTF-8 tuple (game revision, seed, class, mode, contract, difficulty, region) as base64url with an FNV checksum. This detects copying errors, not forgery. Input is bounded to 768 characters, validates catalog references and canonical challenge dates, and cannot contain rewards, scores, equipment or ghosts. Preparation enforces local unlocks; historical daily/weekly dates still select the fixed challenge loadout. Different game revisions show a compatibility notice. Full run and ghost exchange remains the separate JSON format.

The `deploy` power effect creates a neutral, breakable Gravity Machine body and binds a catalog-configured field to its position. It shares the existing ability field authority and construct lifetime cleanup with planets. Capacity checks reserve both body and field slots before spending energy or assigning a cause. Engineer starts with Gravity Beacon and Repulsor; the beacon evolves into an Orbital Engine vortex. New powers participate in the ordinary shop, level-up, codex and mastery catalogs. Replay revision r7 records this catalog/class change.

The shared `TaggedContent` shape separates content identity from modifier target filters. RunBuild collects learned-power, run-relic, equipped-item and active-mutation tags for generic synergy requirements. Unused inventory and unselected mutations contribute nothing. Equipment families now expose their existing family tag; unique gear and mutations declare explicit identities. Build details and equipment codex entries show the resulting tags. Replay revision r8 isolates these synergy changes.

PhysicsWorld emits an optional post-spawn callback after registering a body. Game translates it into a typed `entitySpawned` fact; active expeditions record late-wave enemies and deployed objects, while room construction records initial spawns explicitly. The codex distinguishes encountered guardians from recorded defeats and remembers temporary run equipment without granting permanent ownership. Events, mutations and neutral physical objects have their own discovery sections. Impact classification and orbital mastery victories derive from authored power tags rather than hard-coded power IDs; replay revision r9 captures that gameplay correction.

Difficulty rewards scale chamber currency and XP by a catalog factor before charter currency bonuses, leaving physical pickups and explicit event offers intact. Bosses on Singularity/Infinite tiers lock a trap position and mode when the attack warning begins; phase changes do not move or transform an already telegraphed trap. A missed telegraph suppresses the trap. All traps share the global field budget, expire naturally and are removed on boss death. Replay revision r10 separates these rules from older route ghosts.

Summoned planets and gravity machines preflight every body against the arena bounds, current wall polygons and static entities before creating a cause or spending resources. Binary System checks both placements atomically. Clearance uses actual polygon edges (including rotated/moving walls) without constructing temporary physics bodies; dynamic matter can still be displaced by a successful summon. Replay revision r11 includes placement validation.

Field ownership now uses the final capped acceleration with and without each candidate field. One spatial query accumulates field contributions and suppression factors, then evaluates all removals in linear time; overlapping zero fields and signed responses are supported. Fully suppressed, filtered, centered radial or redundant saturated forces cannot acquire projectile ownership. Among effective power fields, the newest cast owns motion. The full-gameplay benchmark now exercises 220 bodies plus 50 power fields, including attribution, separately from raw solver and lens-math timings. Replay revision r12 includes these causality changes.

Seven additional powers introduce independent timed response factors, exact momentum scaling and physical spring constraints. Response factors refresh by power ID and clean up by body generation; inversion, amplification and phasing compose without overwriting one another. Spring links preflight target counts and a 12-link global ability budget, mark tension-caused motion, expire, and detach when endpoints die, recycle or replace their physics body. The renderer shows link endpoints and signed response labels. The Conductor and Kineticist classes, twelve tradeoff/conditional relics and two family-aware synergies build on these interactions. Replay revision r13 covers this catalog and rule change.

The specialist catalogs bring powers/relics to 50/50. Material- and projectile-filtered fields share the same gravity/attribution query as ordinary powers; self-only response effects target the core explicitly. Binary System evolves into a three-body Solar System with atomic placement and six field slots. Trigger-created relic fields now retain their causal chain for later entrants and preserve Orbit tags in chain metadata; compression damage remains exclusive to primary wells. The full gameplay stress case includes all 220 bodies, 50 power fields and 12 physical links. Replay revision r14 includes these catalogs and field ownership rules.

Power audio uses bounded effect-family arrangements rather than one triangle sweep for every cast: deployables click and settle, tethers pluck paired tones, response powers use opposing sweeps, planets/singularities add a subharmonic, rails use a short bright waveform, and inward/outward launches sweep in opposite directions. Delayed layers count against the same twelve-voice budget as music and effects; mute prevents allocation and ended voices disconnect both oscillator and envelope. Listening and physical-speaker mix validation remain external.

Distant-object dormancy is intentionally limited to stationary, force-free neutral props beyond 450 units from the core after one idle second. Gravity is still sampled every step so field/global changes wake immediately. Nearby props, actors, pickups and constraint endpoints remain awake; impulses, slow collisions and any wall geometry change wake sleeping matter before resolution. Integration and post-step repair work skip unchanged sleepers. Warmed host comparisons for 220 spaced props measured 0.108 ms awake versus 0.064 ms dormant at p95; touching props measured 0.193 versus 0.073 ms. Full 220-body/50-field/12-link gameplay remains around 1.8 ms at p95 on this host, not a mobile performance claim. Replay revision r15 records the new integration policy.

Rail Wardens, Null Shepherds and Salvagers expand regional enemy behavior without spawning conventional weapon damage. A Warden locks a visible aim point before throwing existing neutral matter and postpones an attack when no warning occurred. Shepherd auras damp gravity and disappear on death. Salvagers consume nearby breakable props to heal only after the prop is destroyed, with a cooldown; indestructible rocks are excluded. Their region pools and nine compatible elite variants are authored explicitly. The codex now gives tactical explanations for every base enemy. Replay revision r16 covers these encounters.

Six further behaviors complete the 25-base-enemy alpha target: Momentum Lancers use locked warning lines before self-launches; Flux Mites alternate a composable gravity factor while remaining solid; Brood Husks split into protected motes under the shared brood/body caps; Magnetic Sentinels reverse a single metal-only field; Momentum Brokers mark matter before exchanging velocities; Barricade Weavers create up to three collision-checked movable plates. Brood placement respects solid geometry, broker marks can escape, and plates remain usable matter after their builder dies. Every base enemy has regional encounters and codex counterplay. Replay revision r17 covers the expanded regional pools.

Replay r18 adds ten kinetic/mass powers and ten relics (60 of each), plus three synergies. Temporary density uses independent mass factors over a persistent base shared by classes, elite scaling, room rules and inspector edits. Expiry defers recalculation for static bodies until their lock releases; player rotation lock and projectile pool generations are preserved. Vector turns retain speed and authored angles across levels; steering rejects an undefined aim and only claims changed motion. Orbital impulses, collision-triggered projectile returns and damage-triggered braking manipulate existing bodies. Relic orbital causes and Mass impact tags support their corresponding rewards. Orbital victory recognition now uses actual Orbit-tagged kills, including relic causes, without fabricating power mastery. The mass overlay was reviewed on a phone browser capture; targeted browser and production offline checks pass. Twelve input-only quick seeds finished with ten wins, two defeats and zero stalls, with winning simulation times of 40–131 seconds; human pacing remains open.

Planetfall is integrated in replay r19. The ninth, append-only region supplies broken colony encounters, competing fields, moving ruins, a chapter and hidden memory. Planet Split consumes an owned, movable summon only after checking both child placements and net body/field capacity. Two destructible moons share base mass, copy temporary factors with their original expiries, inherit velocity with opposite separation impulses, and expire no later than their parent; clamped/tiny mass cannot be duplicated. Planetary Endless has a dedicated colony/dead-planet/island route, grants starting planet powers, keeps planetary collision sources alongside event rules, and uses the normal endless research gate, escalation, archive, code and checkpoint systems. The campaign now covers all nine regions. Selection changes immediately refresh power cooldown feedback.
